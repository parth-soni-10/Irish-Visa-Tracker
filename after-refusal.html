After a refusal

Refused: appeal, review or reapply for an Irish visa

General information • Not legal advice • Reviewed 26 November 2026

A refusal is a decision on one application, not a permanent ban. The refusal letter should give the reasons and say whether you have a right of appeal. Read that letter first: appeal routes and deadlines depend on the visa type and the office that decided the application.

1. Read the refusal letter before doing anything else

The letter identifies the reasons and any available remedy. Do not rely on a generic internet template, and do not assume that a missing application number in a published file is a refusal. The tracker records published decisions; it does not decide appeals or hold your file.

If the letter has not arrived, contact the visa office or VFS centre that handled the application. Keep the envelope, email and any reference numbers.

2. Check whether an appeal is available

Short-stay visas: from 1 June 2026, applicants can no longer appeal the refusal of a short-stay visa application, except where the application was made under the EU Free Movement Directive (2004/38/EC). Check the ISD appeal guidance and your letter for the current rule.

Other visa categories: the letter will say whether an appeal is available and how to submit it. Appeal deadlines are strict. Address every reason given in the refusal; additional documents must usually be submitted with the appeal. An appeal is not a guarantee of a different outcome.

Office-specific instructions can differ. Check the ISD visa offices page and the office that issued the decision. If you are unsure about a deadline, contact that office rather than waiting.

3. Reapplying is a new application

If you reapply, you will usually need a new application, a new fee and a new decision. Address the reasons in the earlier refusal and provide the evidence that was missing or unclear. Do not submit false or altered documents. A new application does not automatically preserve an appeal right or a place in a queue.

If your circumstances have changed, explain the change with evidence. The tracker cannot tell you whether a new application will succeed.

Office differences

Appeals and queries are handled by the office that decided the application. These pages link to each office's published decisions and official source:

  • New Delhi visa decisions India, Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka
  • Beijing visa decisions China, Hong Kong, Macau and Mongolia
  • Abu Dhabi visa decisions UAE, Qatar, Gulf states and North Africa
  • Abuja visa decisions Nigeria, Ghana, Côte d’Ivoire and Senegal
  • Ankara visa decisions Türkiye, Azerbaijan and Saudi residents
  • Dublin visa decisions Pakistan and applications transferred to Dublin

Official sources

  • ISD: appeal a negative decision — who can appeal, how to submit and what happens next.
  • ISD visa decisions — processing and decision information.
  • ISD visa offices — the office that handles your country or application.
  • Tracker processing times — current published queues, with caveats.

This page is general information, not immigration or legal advice. Appeal deadlines and rights are set by the official decision and the applicable rules; confirm them with the issuing office.
