Irish Visa Processing Times - What the Published Data Shows

Irish Visa Decision Tracker • Updated 7 November 2026 • Unofficial, not immigration advice

There is no single processing time for an Irish visa, and no honest way to promise one. What the official publishers release - and what this tracker records - is a queue date: the application date an office or scheme has reached. The gap between that date and today is the lag. This page shows what those queues are doing, how to read a change in the right direction, and what none of it can tell you about an individual case.

Queue date, lag, and why the difference matters

  • Queue date - the application date the office says it has reached. Anything received after it has not been reached yet.
  • Lag - the distance between the queue date and the date the figure was published. It is a property of the queue, not of your application.
  • Change - the same figure measured twice. A growing lag means the queue is falling further behind; a shrinking lag means it is catching up.

A queue date that has not moved for a week is not a mistake. Published dates often sit still and then jump, and a day with no published file is a closure, not a day of zero decisions.

What the tracker currently records

These figures change as offices publish. The linked pages have the latest numbers.

  • Stamp 1G - ISD is processing applications received on 28 September 2026, a lag of 40 days. See Stamp 1G processing dates.
  • Employment permits - across the seven published categories, the shortest queue is 6 days and the longest is 192 days. See employment permit processing dates.
  • Embassy decisions, New Delhi - the record holds 42,381 published decisions across 69 published days, of which 83.9% were approvals. See daily decision summaries.

The approval share covers decisions already published, not the chances for a particular application.

When should I submit?

Pick the category that matches your application and the date you need a decision by. The planner subtracts the current queue lag to show the latest application date the published queue supports.

This is queue arithmetic, not a processing promise. Apply earlier if your travel or documents require it, and check the official ISD page before relying on a date.

What the data shows right now

A queue date on its own says little, so this is the same figures measured thirty days apart. These are the tracker's own observations, as of 7 November 2026, and each one describes a direction rather than a prediction.

Stamp 1G. The overall queue moved from 30 days behind on 8 October 2026 to 40 days behind on 7 November 2026 - it fell 10 days further behind in a month.

Employment permits. Reviews moved from 163 days behind on 8 October 2026 to 192 days on 7 November 2026, while Renewals improved from 83 days behind to 65. Queues inside the same scheme are moving in opposite directions.

Decision volume. The last six published days carried 1,174 decisions, against 1,283 across the six published days before them. Fewer decisions being published is not the same as more refusals.

What a published queue cannot tell you

  • When your own case will be decided. A case can be decided before or after the published date; the date describes the queue, not the file.
  • That a missing application number means refusal. It means the decision has not been published, the application sits with a different office, or that day's file has not been published.
  • Which office has your file. Some categories are transferred between offices; the ISD list of visa offices explains which office handles which countries.
  • Anything about an individual outcome. Nothing on this site can see, predict or influence a decision.

Why keeping the history matters

Official publishers show only the current processing date, so their pages cannot show whether a queue is moving faster than last month. This tracker keeps each observation, which makes that direction visible. The underlying data is public: /data/stamp_1g.json, /data/work_permits.json and /data/visa_decisions.json.

Official sources

For authoritative figures, use the official pages:

  • ISD visa decisions and processing times - the official published dates for visa applications and appeals.
  • ISD visa offices - which office handles which countries and applications.
  • Embassy of Ireland in India - contact details for the embassy that publishes the New Delhi decision lists.

DETE publishes its own employment-permit processing dates on enterprise.gov.ie. If a figure here disagrees with the official page, the official page is correct - please report the difference.
